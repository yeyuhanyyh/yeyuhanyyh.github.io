A sharp lower bound for gradient descent on smooth strongly convex functions

For smooth strongly convex objectives, Kaizhao Liu and I proved a lower bound for GD with predetermined nonnegative stepsizes. The exponent of κ in that bound was 1/psil − o(1), where psil = log2(1 + √2). This working draft extends our previous paper. It removes the o(1) term from the exponent and drops the nonnegativity assumption on the stepsizes.

How the proof developed

I asked GPT-6.2 to revisit our construction in light of Jeong and Hong’s smooth convex lower bound. For negative steps, I suggested an idea inspired by resisting oracles. Nemirovsky and Yudin (1979) used this idea in their classical lower bound, and Drori later used it to prove the exact lower bound attained by the optimized gradient method (OGM). Could a large gradient in a direction hidden from the earlier iterates make a negative step carry GD far from the minimizer?

A second question led more directly to the proof. Could GD stay on one coordinate through a stretch of short positive steps and negative steps, then switch at a sufficiently large positive step? The draft does this on a quadratic coordinate.
